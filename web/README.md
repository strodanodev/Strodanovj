# Strodano VJ - web edition

Runs in Chrome or Edge with nothing to install or compile.

**Run it:** double-click `index.html`. **Change it:** edit a file, press F5.

- `shaders.js` - built-in effects and generators (add an entry, refresh, it appears in the lists)
- `engine.js` - WebGL2 engine: clips, mixing, effect chains, output mapping, save/load
- `mediafolder.js` - remembered media folder: clips relink from it by file name on every start
- `midi.js` - MIDI control and learn mode
- `keys.js` - keyboard shortcuts (press **?** or click **Keys** for the list)
- `motion.js` - beat-synced LFOs on parameters, and clip transition lengths
- `camera.js`, `text.js`, `smart3d.js` - camera clips and camera input, text clips, 3D generators (three.js, in `vendor/`)
- `hands.js`, `inputs.js` - hand tracking and gestures (MediaPipe, in `vendor/mediapipe/`), and the input picker on every slider
- `analysis.js`, `audio.js`, `crate.js`, `mix.js`, `score.js`, `segments.js`, `cues.js`, `follow.js`, `dj.js` - DJ mode (and `report.js`, the analysis report page): track analysis, the two decks and the music clock, the track list, auto-mix and autopilot, and the deck panel
- `ui.js` - interface and main loop
- `index.html` - layout and styles

Inside the app, **Shader Editor** compiles custom effects and generators as you type, and loads single-pass ISF shaders.

**Media folder...** (Files tab) remembers a folder of clips. After a restart the browser may ask for permission again: click **Reconnect**.

**MIDI** (menu bar) turns on learn mode: click a fader, clip, column, button, effect parameter or generator parameter, then move a knob or press a pad. Esc finishes. Mappings are kept in the browser, listed in the Composition tab, and work for every composition. Effect and generator mappings follow the effect when the chain is reordered, and survive Save / Open.

**Keys:** 1-9, 0 trigger clips on the selected layer (Shift: whole column), Up/Down pick the layer, B / S / X bypass, solo, eject, Left/Right move the crossfader (Shift: all the way), Space taps tempo, R resyncs.

**LFOs:** click **~** next to any effect or generator slider to move it with the beat: pick a shape (sine, triangle, saw, square, random), how long one cycle lasts (1/4 beat to 4 bars) and the depth. The slider stays the centre value. LFOs follow tap tempo, or the music while a DJ deck plays, and are saved with the composition.

**Transitions:** in the Layer tab, **Transition** sets how a newly triggered clip replaces the old one (Fade, Wipe right / left / up / down, Dissolve, Luma, Through black, Zoom, Iris) and how long it takes (Cut, or 1/4 beat to 4 bars).

**Camera** (Sources tab): drag **Camera clip** onto a slot to show a live camera. **Use as input** analyses the camera so the show reacts to the performer: how much is moving, where, and how bright it is. Effects marked **(cam)** follow it by themselves (Follow Spot, Motion Glitch, Motion Zoom, Camera Key, Camera Displace, Camera Mix), and any slider can follow it: click **~** and pick Camera motion, Camera X, Camera Y or Camera light. Shaders read `cam` (motion, x, y, light) and `camTex`.

**Text** (Sources tab): titles, names or lyrics, one line per cue. Lines move on with **N** (Shift+N goes back), Next line, or automatically every beat / bar / phrase. Animations: pulse on beat, kick bounce, typewriter, scroll, one word per beat, strobe, fade in. Size, position, colour and outline are sliders, so they take LFOs and MIDI.

**3D** (Sources tab): Wire Sphere, Particle Storm, Cube Tunnel, Wave Grid, Neon Knot, Star Warp, Crystal Field, Orbit Rings, Audio Blob and Hand Comet, made with three.js. They follow the beat and the music; their camera and hand sliders react to the camera input and your hands. A new project starts with 4 of them, picked at random.

**Camera on a layer:** in the Sources tab, **Show on a layer** adds a top layer called Camera, playing the camera, ready for effects, transitions and blend modes.

**Hands** (Sources tab): **Track hands** follows your hands and fingers on the camera (a few seconds to load the first time; it runs in the background so the show keeps its frame rate). The preview shows the tracked points and the recognised gesture; the line under it shows how many times a second the hands are analysed (aim for 15 or more) and how sure the tracker is. If hands are slow to be found or get lost, raise **Sensitivity**; if it sees hands that are not there, lower it. Good, even light on your hands helps more than anything else.
- Sliders: hand X / Y, pinch, open hand, closeness and two hands apart.
- Gestures work like pads: open palm, fist, point up, V sign, thumbs up, thumbs down, rock on. Turn on **Map** (the MIDI button), click a clip or button, then make the gesture.

**Making a slider follow something:** every effect / generator / text / 3D slider has a chip next to it (**~** when nothing drives it). Click it and pick what it follows: a beat LFO, the music, the camera, your hands, or a MIDI knob. Every choice shows a live level so you can see what is moving. Or press **Detect** and do the movement: it picks the source that moved most. **Follow it** sends the slider exactly where the input is (best for positions); **Push up** adds the input on top of the slider; **Invert** flips it.

**DJ** (menu bar) opens two decks. Drop an audio file on a deck (or press Load): the tempo and beat grid are found automatically, and while a track plays the visuals follow its beat instead of the tapped tempo. **Sync** (on by default) matches a deck's tempo to the other deck and lines the bars up when you press Play. The A/B crossfader blends the two decks together with the layers set to A and B. Shaders can read the music: `bar`, `phrase`, `audio.xyzw` (bass, mid, high, level) and `kick`; try the **Audio Pulse** effect and the **Audio Rings** source. Changing a deck's tempo changes its pitch (no key lock yet).

**Mixing for beginners:** drop a folder's worth of tracks on **Tracks** (or use a media folder): each one's tempo, key (Camelot) and energy are worked out once and remembered. While a track plays, the list ranks the others as the next track (Great / Good / OK / Hard, hover for why). Load one into the other deck and press **Mix**: on the next phrase it brings the new track in, in time, with the chosen style (Smooth blend, Bass swap, Echo out, Cut) and length, and the crossfader moves the A/B layers with it. Tracks too far apart in tempo get an echo out instead of a blend. Waveforms mark **MIX IN** and **OUTRO**, and the deck header counts down to the outro. **Autopilot** plays the list by itself, mixing each track in at the previous one's outro. **Clips on bar / beat** makes clip launches wait for the music; **Auto-gain** plays every track at the same loudness; **Own tempo after mix** lets a matched track drift back to its own tempo. Moving the crossfader during a mix hands it to you.

**Song sections and section looks:** every analysed track is split into intro, build, drop, breakdown and outro, shown as a coloured band under each waveform; the deck header counts down to the next drop. Set up the visuals you want for a drop, then press **Drop** in the **Section looks** row (or **Save**); do the same for the other sections and turn on **Auto-score**. Each look then comes back on the first bar of that section in every track, gliding in over the chosen time. Any slider's **~** button can also follow the music instead of a beat LFO: **Kick**, **Bass**, **Mids**, **Highs**, **Loudness**, **Build-up** (rises through each build) or **In drop** (up during drops). Section looks are saved with the composition.

**Editing segments:** press **Edit segments...** in the Section looks row to fix a track's sections by hand. Drag a boundary on the waveform to move it (it snaps to bars), or pick a segment and make it longer or shorter by 1 or 4 bars (the next segment gives or takes the bars), split it in half or at the playhead, merge it with a neighbour, or change what it is (intro, build, drop, breakdown, outro). For each segment, choose its visuals: the look of its type (the default), the look of another type, **its own look** (set up the visuals and press **Use current visuals**), or leave the visuals as they are. With Auto-score on, each segment's visuals come in on its first bar. **Preview** shows a segment's look now, **Play from here** plays the deck from its start, and **Back to analysed** forgets the changes. Edits belong to the track and are saved with the composition.

**Track scores:** play a track, press **Rec** in the **Track score** row and perform: every clip, column, fader and effect slider you touch (mouse or MIDI controller) is kept against that track's beats, and plays back every time the track plays, at any tempo, from any point, in any mix. Record again to overdub (only what you touch is replaced); **Undo pass** takes the last pass back. Cues show as ticks on the waveform. Scores are saved with the composition. **During a mix:** save a **Mix** look in the Section looks row to have it come in while an auto-mix runs, and use **Mix progress** or **Mid-mix** on any slider's **~** button to move it with the transition. **External gear:** send MIDI clock from CDJs / Traktor / a drum machine and the visuals follow it when no deck plays. Or turn on **Line-in** and **Recognise tracks**: once a track from the list has played for a while (usually 30-60 s, across a section change) it is recognised with its position, so its sections, section looks and track score run as if it were on a deck. Recognition is a best guess and shows how sure it is; **Forget** drops it.

**Checking the analysis on your own music:** open `report.html` (next to `index.html`) and drop a folder of tracks on it. It runs the same analysis DJ mode uses and lists each track's tempo, beat grid confidence, key, loudness, mix-in and outro points and sections. Click a track to see its waveform with bars, sections and mix points, and listen with **Beat clicks** (a click on every beat it found, higher on the first beat of each bar). Mark what is wrong, then **Export report** saves a JSON file with the analysis and your marks, for tuning the analysis.

**Agent** (the robot in the sidebar) is an autopilot that runs **your composition**: it plays the clips you set up on your layers, with the music. The quickest way in: put clips on your layers (or press **Add starter clips** in the Agent panel for a ready-made set you can change), open the Agent, drop your songs on the **Playlist** (it replaces the clip grid while the Agent is open; the **Layers** tab brings the grid back), pick a **Vibe** (Chill, Club or Festival) and press **Start**. The songs play in playlist order, each mixed into the next, while the Agent directs your layers: it chooses which clip each layer plays and fades layers in and out with the energy (calm parts keep only the bottom layer up, peaks bring them all in), never above the level you set on a layer. Columns you tag **Calm / Groove / Peak / Drop** (under More options) are played as finished looks, exactly as you built them, when the music fits; untagged clips are combined by the Agent. Your layer transitions and effects apply as you set them. Each song shows its waveform with its parts (intro, build, drop, breakdown) and the scene the Agent has planned for each part from your clips: click one to get a different combination. Plans follow your grid: change a clip and they are made again. Drag the **IN** and **OUT** markers to choose where a song comes in and where the mix into the next song begins (they snap to bars; double-click resets), and between two songs pick the mix style and how the visuals change over (with the mix, a quick fade, or a cut). Drag a song by its handle to reorder. **Lock** a layer (playlist header, or the lock button on a layer in the grid) and the Agent, section looks and track scores leave it alone, so you can play it yourself; bypassed layers are left alone too. **Next song** mixes into the next song on the next phrase. Under **More options** you can make it listen to a **microphone / line-in** (music from any player or a DJ booth) or a **music file** (MP3, WAV, M4A, or a recorded mix as MP4, even an hour long) instead, and set how often it varies. It changes scene when the song changes, cuts to the drop scene on each drop (with analysed songs it sees the drop coming and takes the picture nearly black for the last two beats), goes calm in breakdowns, and varies every few bars, even with no music playing. **New scene**, **Hold** and **Drop!** steer it. Trigger any clip yourself and that layer comes up and the Agent steps aside for 8 bars. **Stop** gives your layers back at the levels you set.

**Record** saves the output with its sound as a video file (MP4 in current Chrome and Edge, otherwise WebM), up to one hour, written straight to disk. **Render music file** plays the open file from the start with the Agent directing and saves the whole thing; **Render playlist** does the same with the playlist mixed in order. Rendering runs in real time: keep the window visible, or open the output window, until it finishes.

Limits of running in a browser: video codecs are whatever the browser plays (H.264 MP4/MOV yes; ProRes, HAP and DXV no), FFGL plugins cannot load, and without a media folder the clips must be relinked after a reload by adding the same files again.
